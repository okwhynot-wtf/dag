# The minimal additional axiom for Hilbert-space structure

What must be added to the spine to force the propositions of the theory to be
those of a Hilbert space?

This document reports the answer reached in `formal/quantum/` (seven modules, 65
audited results). It is a fork-only development: nothing in the paper-cited
chain imports it.

## Short answer

**There is no single additional axiom, and the reason is precise.** The spine
admits two natural readings as an orthogonality space. One has rank at most
two; the other has unbounded rank and is still classical. So rank — the
obvious single thing to add — repairs neither reading on its own, and what has
to be added is a conjunction.

The minimal *sufficient* addition is a four-clause hypothesis, `Axiom.Orthoframe`:

1. **Irredundancy** — distinct states are separated by orthogonality. Without
   it, duplicating every point buys "superposition" for free and the whole
   hypothesis is satisfied by a classical model.
2. **Superposition** — any two exclusive states span a subspace containing a
   third state distinct from both.
3. **Orthomodularity** — the lattice of closed subspaces satisfies Piron's
   compatibility law.
4. **Same size** — some infinite orthogonal family has all its members of one
   size, expressed as a symmetry condition so that no scalars are named.

Rank is *not* a separate clause: the same-size clause has an infinite
orthogonal family inside it, so rank of every order is read off it
(`Axiom.rank_of_normed`). Piron's irreducibility hypothesis is likewise not
assumed, following from superposition by a cited equivalence — but that is a
discharge, not a saving: the work is imported rather than avoided.

The clause carrying the quantum content is **superposition**; it is the one
that fails for a classical system.

## The starting position

Four groups of facts about the spine. Every Lean result cited in this section
is machine-checked and axiom-free; the sentences that go beyond what a cited
theorem states are marked as such.

**The spine is classical at the fundamental layer, provably.** Given two
distinct elements of a fundamental carrier there is no third
(`Classical.no_third_state`); with `Classical.fundamental_has_two_states`,
which produces the two, that is "exactly two". Superposition asks for a state
that is neither of two given alternatives, and the spine *refutes* that request
rather than leaving it open. Predicates on that carrier are determined by their
two values, and every element of the four-element Boolean algebra is realised
(`bitOfPred_surjective`, under `DecidableEq`). That is a correspondence of
underlying sets; the ortholattice isomorphism is not constructed.

**The reading is not a form.** `Ladder.rep 0` is `Tower.f₀ = fun a _ => a`, the
first projection, so `rep` is asymmetric at every level
(`SpineFacts.rep_not_symmetric`, `rep_not_symmetric_at`). It is therefore not a
Hermitian form; that *no* reading definable over the spine could be one is not
claimed.

**The carriers are small.** The spine's level-size counter gives `k+2` at rung
`k` — arithmetic about a `Nat` recursion, not a cardinality theorem about the
type `Ladder.Level k`, which the spine nowhere proves. The colimit contains a
copy of `Nat` (`SpineFacts.omega_stage_injective`) and every element of it is a
base point or a stage (`Classical.omega_is_base_or_stage`); countability
follows informally. A Hilbert space of dimension ≥ 2 has a continuum of pure
states.

**The complex-structure shortcut is closed.** A complex structure needs `J`
with `J ∘ J = act`. No such `J` exists on the fundamental carrier
(`SpineFacts.no_sqrt_on_fundamental`), and more sharply, none exists on *any*
inhabited carrier generated by a live act
(`SpineFacts.no_sqrt_of_generated`) — the demand contradicts monism itself. It
becomes consistent as soon as generation is dropped: a four-element model is
given (`SpineFacts.sqrt_model_exists`), where the quarter-turn squares to the
componentwise flip.

## The obstruction

The spine supplies an orthogonality space **for free**. `Orbit.Step act x y` is
`y = act x`; `Orbit.SymmetricStep` makes it symmetric and `Orbit.Live` makes it
irreflexive, which is exactly an orthoset. No axiom is needed for that.

But `Step` is the graph of a *function*, so no state has two distinct
orthogonal partners and no three states are pairwise orthogonal
(`Orthoset.act_orthoset_has_no_triple`). That is an upper bound — rank at most
two; no matching lower bound is proved, so "exactly two" is not claimed. The
cap comes from orthogonality being induced by a single act, **not** from the
monism clause, and rank cannot be raised while orthogonality is the step
relation of one act (`Orthoset.rank3_not_from_one_act`).

The other natural reading is distinguishability, `x ≠ y`. On any carrier with
an injective `Nat`-indexed family — the colimit has one, by `Limit.stage_inj` —
that relation has rank `n` for every `n`
(`Orthoset.discrete_rank_of_injective`), so the spine is *not* short of rank
under every reading. It fails for the opposite reason: the closure of a set
adds nothing there, so no third state lies in the span of two, and
superposition fails at any size (`Axiom.discrete_no_superposition`).

| reading | rank | superposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Orbit.Step act`, on a fundamental carrier | fails, at most 2 (`act_orthoset_has_no_triple`) | fails — only two states exist, so no pair has a third in its span (`Classical.no_third_state`) |
| distinguishability | holds, unbounded (`discrete_rank_of_injective`) | fails (`discrete_no_superposition`) |

Two qualifications, since the "different directions" story is easy to
overstate. First, on the spine's *fundamental* carrier the act reading fails
both clauses, not only rank. Second, on a larger carrier with a live involution
the act reading's superposition clause becomes trivially true — the polar of
`{x, act x}` is empty, so its double polar is everything — which is exactly the
degenerate satisfaction that irredundancy exists to exclude.

What the table does establish is the load-bearing point: **rank alone is not
the missing ingredient.** One reading has unbounded rank and is still not
quantum. So no single clause about rank repairs both readings, and that is the
answer to "why not one axiom".

## Minimality accounting

**Two of the four clauses are shown independent. Two are not.** Stating that
plainly matters more than the headline reads well.

Shown, in Lean:

- **Superposition cannot be dropped.** `Witness.superposition_independent_of_the_rest`
  exhibits the classical orthoset on `Nat` satisfying irredundancy,
  orthomodularity, the normed infinite orthogonal family, and rank of every
  order — while failing superposition. That is a proper independence witness:
  it satisfies *every* other clause. (An earlier version of this document cited
  the classical bit instead, which does not do the job, since the bit fails
  rank as well. The witness module uses excluded middle for the orthomodularity
  check and records it in the audit.)
- **The spine's own orthoset fails the hypothesis.** It has no orthogonal
  triple, so it carries no infinite orthogonal family
  (`Axiom.spine_is_not_orthoframe`, via `Orthoset.act_orthoset_has_no_triple`).
- **Rank alone is not enough.** The classical orthoset has rank `n` for every
  `n` (`Orthoset.discrete_rank_of_injective`) and still fails superposition
  (`Axiom.discrete_no_superposition`).

Argued but not formalised:

- **Irredundancy cannot be dropped.** Duplicate every point of the classical
  orthoset on `Nat`: the duplicate of `x` lies in the double polar of any
  orthogonal pair containing `x`, so superposition holds for free while the
  model stays classical. This is why the clause is there — an earlier version
  of the hypothesis omitted it and was satisfiable by exactly this model.

Not shown at all:

- **Orthomodularity has no independence witness here.** Nothing rules out its
  being derivable from the other clauses in this signature. It is in
  `Orthoframe` because the imported theorem wants it.
- **The same-size clause has no witness here**, though the literature supplies
  one: Keller (1980) constructs infinite-dimensional orthomodular spaces over
  non-Archimedean fields that have infinite orthogonal families of atoms and
  are not Hilbert spaces, so the clause cannot be weakened to bare infinite
  orthogonality. Cited, not formalised.

Separately, Piron's conclusion alone is not a Hilbert space — his hypotheses do
not pin the coordinatising ring, which is what Solèr's theorem was introduced
to settle. See Holland's survey, §2.

**A correction worth recording.** An earlier version of this development
declared `superposition_iff_irreducible` without the covering law. That
statement is *false*: the horizontal sum of two copies of the eight-element
Boolean algebra is complete, atomistic, orthomodular and irreducible, yet two
atoms in the same block join to an element covering only themselves, so
superposition fails. Since every symbol in that declaration is fully defined in
Lean, the false axiom made `False` derivable. The covering law is now a
hypothesis. Likewise `Orthoframe` originally omitted irredundancy and was
satisfied by a classical model. Both were found by adversarial review, not by
the build — a declared axiom is only as good as its citation.

Expressing the same-size clause without naming a form is what makes the
hypothesis stateable at all in this signature. Solèr's own condition is that a
Hermitian form take the same value on each member of an infinite orthogonal
sequence — but the form does not exist until *after* the representation theorem
has been applied. `Orthoset.SameSize` replaces it with: some symmetry carries
one point to the other and fixes everything orthogonal to both.

That substitution is a **surrogate, not an equivalence**, and only one
direction is clear. Two orthogonal vectors of equal norm are exchanged by an
isometry fixing their common orthocomplement, so equal norm gives the symmetry.
The converse does not follow: an automorphism of an orthogonality space need
only preserve orthogonality, and may be induced by a similarity — a semilinear
map rescaling the form — carrying no information about norms. Closing that gap
is a Wigner/Uhlhorn-type step, and it is neither proved nor assumed here. The
strength of `Axiom.piron_soler_orthoset`, which consumes this condition, should
be read with that in mind.

## Is the hypothesis satisfiable?

Yes, so the derivation is not vacuous. Take the rays of a separable complex
Hilbert space with the usual orthogonality. Rank holds; the double polar of an
orthogonal pair is the ray set of their two-dimensional span, which contains
the ray of their sum, giving superposition; orthomodularity is the Hilbert
decomposition `T = S ⊕ (T ∩ S^⊥)`; and for an orthonormal basis the unitary
transposing two members fixes the rest pointwise, giving the same-size
condition. That model cannot be built in Lean core — there is no `ℂ` here — so
this is an informal consistency note, not a theorem.

One further scope note. `Axiom.SuperpositionO` quantifies over **orthogonal**
pairs, whereas Piron's superposition principle quantifies over any two distinct
atoms. The restriction is formally weaker; it should be harmless given the
other clauses, since one atom can be replaced by its component orthogonal to
the other without changing the span, but that reduction is not proved here.

## What is proved, and what is cited

57 of the package's 65 results carry no axioms at all. Four of its seven modules —
`SpineFacts`, `Ortho`, `Orthoset`, `Classical` — are entirely clean, and that
is where the negative content lives: the independence results, the
countermodels, the rank cap, and the polarity facts.

The 8 results that are not clean are **not** the imported theorems themselves;
they are the results stated downstream of them, in `Target` and `Axiom`. They
comprise the two assemblies (`hilbert_of_orthoframe`, `hilbert_of_superposed`,
`piron_soler`), the two discharges (`rank_of_ladder`,
`irreducible_of_superposed`), and `bit_is_not_superposed` — which is a negative
result, but mentions `Superposed` and so inherits the opaque predicate in one
of its field types. The remaining two are the countermodel module's
orthomodularity check and the summary that uses it, which need excluded middle.
So the slogan "everything negative is clean" is *almost* true and is not stated
here as a rule; `formal/AXIOMS.md` and the `#print
axioms` blocks give the exact footprints.

Everything that reaches a Hilbert space is cited. Four representation theorems
are declared as *named Lean axioms* rather than proved, alongside four opaque
predicates (`IsGeneralizedHilbert`, `HasOrthonormalSequence`,
`IsHilbertLattice`, `IsHilbertOrthoset`) whose content those citations fix —
eight declarations in all. `#print axioms` on any downstream result names them,
and `formal/AXIOMS.md` separates proof from citation in its **declared**
column:

- `Target.piron` — Piron, *Axiomatique quantique*, Helv. Phys. Acta 37 (1964).
- `Target.soler` — Solèr, *Characterization of Hilbert spaces by orthomodular
  spaces*, Comm. Algebra 23 (1995). Survey: Holland, Bull. AMS 32 (1995).
- `Axiom.piron_soler_orthoset` — the orthoset form. Assembled from the above
  plus the orthogonality-space tradition (Dacey; Foulis–Randall; Vetterlein,
  J. Geom. 110, 2019); no single paper states it in this form.
- `Axiom.superposition_iff_irreducible` — Piron; Beltrametti–Cassinelli (1981),
  for AC lattices. The covering law is required; see the correction above.

## The wall

Two walls, one soft and one hard.

**Soft — the toolchain.** Lean core without Mathlib has no `ℝ`, no `ℂ`, no
division rings, no completeness and no analysis. Piron and Solèr cannot be
proved in this corpus; they can only be stated and imported. Building enough
algebra to state Solèr's hypothesis natively would mean a `*`-division ring, a
module over it and a Hermitian form — a substantial development that would not
change the answer, only its bookkeeping.

**Hard — the trichotomy.** Even granted the full hypothesis, Piron plus Solèr
reaches `ℝ`, `ℂ` *or* `ℍ`. Selecting `ℂ` requires a further, independent
condition, and the standard one is local tomography — the state space of a
composite is determined by its parts (Araki 1980; Wootters; Hardy's `K_AB =
K_A K_B`; CDP Axiom 4). **The spine has no product type anywhere**: `grep` for
`Prod` or `×` across all 25 spine modules returns nothing. There is no
composition, no tensor and no notion of a joint system, so there is nothing for
a tomography axiom to attach to.

So the residue is exact. The minimal addition reaches "real, complex or
quaternionic". Pinning `ℂ` is not a matter of finding a cleverer axiom over
this spine — it needs a composition operation the spine does not have, and
building one is a separate development from the one reported here.
