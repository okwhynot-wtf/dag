A Machine-Checked Formal Metaphysics of an Involutive Arena}

\begin{abstract}
We develop a formal metaphysics for an \emph{involutive arena}: a pointed
carrier equipped with a fixed-point-free involution. Within this ambient,
the two-point carrier with Boolean negation is initial and unique up to
label swap; a Lawvere-style diagonal argument yields an incompleteness
(``seal'') with no appeal to arithmetisation; and a single
$\Ztwo$-underdetermination appears in three faces---pole, address, and
channel. Transferring losslessness from the arena to forward-composing
microsteps is discharged by factoring finite injective endomaps as
products of two involutions. Canonicity of the factorisation fails: the
absolute position of the two ``mirrors'' is conventional. What is real is
their exchange, which covers step reversal. We introduce a demarcation
between \emph{content} gauges and \emph{packaging} gauges that keeps this
non-uniqueness from inflating the dictionary's single $\Ztwo$. The
development is machine-checked in Lean~4 without Mathlib and without
$\mathtt{sorry}$. Physics appears only as optional dictionary certificates,
not as a coronation of the metaphysics.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:}
formal metaphysics; involution; Lawvere diagonal; underdetermination;
gauge; machine-checked mathematics.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Mathematical philosophy often proceeds by isolating a small formal
structure and reading it metaphysically. The present paper does that for
one structure---an involutive self-map on a pointed carrier---and then
asks how much of a larger ``arena'' can be derived, transferred, and
honestly refused.

Three results organise the argument.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \textbf{Canon.} Among pointed involutive acts, $(\Bool,\neg)$ is
initial. Fundamentality coincides with reading as negation; uniqueness
holds up to label swap.
\item \textbf{Seal.} No self-reading is point-surjective onto the
predicates of its own level. Incompleteness is semantic (Lawvere), not
arithmetised (G\"odel).
\item \textbf{One underdetermination, three faces.} Pole swap, address
reversal, and channel swap are presentations of a single $\Ztwo$. Channel
\emph{labels} are conventional; channel \emph{swap} is proved content: it
covers step reversal.
\end{enumerate}

A fourth result is the hinge between arena and dynamics. Spine acts are
involutions; ledger microsteps compose forward. The interface demand is
that global injectivity of a joint step $U$ be the transfer of in-arena
losslessness. That transfer is discharged by a classical finite fact:
injective endomaps of $\mathrm{Fin}\,n$ factor as $\sigma\comp\phi$ with
both factors involutions (``two-bounce''). Canonicity of $(\phi,\sigma)$
fails; the failure is not a second dictionary $\Ztwo$ but a
\emph{packaging} gauge---spent inside the discharge, never exported to
consumers of $U$.

The metaphysical signature that emerges is relational throughout:
absolute positions (which pole, which mirror, which channel label) are
unreal; exchanges and relations are real. The paper's contribution is to
make that signature theorem-shaped, machine-checked, and demarcated
against gauge inflation.

\paragraph{Scope and method.}
We present mathematics with a metaphysical reading. Continuum physics,
host-logic incompleteness, and experiential claims are refused.
Accompanying Lean declarations are cited by name; the paper is
self-contained at the level of statements. The formal corpus uses Lean~4
with no Mathlib dependency and no $\mathtt{sorry}$.

\paragraph{Positioning.}
Relative to Quinean underdetermination, the present $\Ztwo$ is narrower
and harder: not ``theory vs world,'' but a proved two-element symmetry
internal to the arena, with a proved exchange and a failed canonicity
attempt for absolute labels. Relative to structural realism, the moral
is similar---relations over absolutes---but obtained as a theorem package
rather than a stance. Relative to formal metaphysics in the modal or
mereological style, the primitives here are dynamical (an act) rather
than static (worlds, parts).

\section{The involutive arena and the canon}
\label{sec:arena}

\begin{definition}[Arena data]
Fix a type $\alpha$ and a map $a:\alpha\to\alpha$. Write
\[
  \mathsf{Step}(a)(x,y)\ :\Leftrightarrow\ y=a(x).
\]
Call $a$ a \emph{symmetric step} when $\mathsf{Step}(a)$ is a symmetric
relation; equivalently, $a\comp a=\mathrm{id}$ (an involution). Call $a$
\emph{live} when it has no fixed point: $\forall x.\, a(x)\neq x$. Call
$a$ \emph{lossless} when it is injective.
\end{definition}

Pointwise, an act either rests ($a(x)=x$), closes a two-cycle
($a(x)\neq x$ and $a(a(x))=x$), or carries an asymmetric step
($a(a(x))\neq x$). Erasure---distinct states with a common
successor---is the pairwise defect of losslessness.

Why involutions? Because the ambient is chosen to exclude smuggled
time and smuggled loss at once. An asymmetric step is already an arrow;
an erasing step is already a collapse. Symmetric step is the equation
that bans both. The metaphysics is therefore conditional: \emph{if} one
works inside an involutive arena, \emph{then} the theorems below apply.
The facticity of that ``if'' remains residue (Section~\ref{sec:refuse}).

\begin{definition}[Pointed unoriented acts]
Objects are triples $(\alpha,a,z)$ with $a$ a symmetric step and
$z:\alpha$ a seed. Morphisms are pointed equivariant maps.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Canon / initiality]
\label{thm:canon}
In the ambient of pointed unoriented acts, $(\Bool,\neg,\mathsf{true})$
is initial. An act is \emph{fundamental} precisely when it is live,
symmetric, and generated from a seed by the two-cycle reading; every
fundamental reads as $\neg$; any two fundamentals correspond uniquely
up to the pointed reading.
\end{theorem}

Thus fundamentality is not an extra primitive: it is initiality plus the
reading theorems. The excluded fixed point (``void'') is just liveness.
Involutivity simultaneously banishes asymmetric arrow and erasure at the
base: the same equation $a\comp a=\mathrm{id}$ yields both.

\begin{proposition}[In-arena losslessness]
\label{prop:no-erase}
If $a$ is a symmetric step, then $a$ is not erasing.
\end{proposition}

This is the spine's own losslessness theorem. It is \emph{not} a second
creed beside involutivity; it is a corollary. The metaphysical reading is
accordingly modest: inside an involutive arena, collapse of distinct
states under the act is impossible. What happens when one steps
``upstairs'' to forward-composing dynamics is the subject of
Section~\ref{sec:transfer}.

\section{Seal: incompleteness without arithmetisation}
\label{sec:seal}

Lawvere's fixed-point argument yields incompleteness from a Cartesian
closed structure and a weakly point-surjective morphism, without coding
syntax as numbers. The arena supplies a concrete instance.

\begin{theorem}[Seal on a fundamental]
\label{thm:seal}
Let $a$ be live. No map $f:A\to (A\to\Bool)$ is point-surjective: the
diagonal dodge $x\mapsto \neg(f\,x\,x)$ is missed. Specialising to
fundamental acts, every self-reading admits an escape.
\end{theorem}

A companion result at corpus grain states that no self-reading of the
formal corpus is universal for the predicates of its own level
(\texttt{corpus\_not\_universal}): names are outnumbered by predicates;
any completed self-portrait lags. Completeness-in-the-limit is a
\emph{policy} (a fair schedule of self-readings), not a theorem that the
corpus catches itself.

\begin{remark}
The seal is not a claim that Lean cannot prove its own consistency, nor
a G\"odel arithmetisation of the corpus. Those are refused as category
errors for this project. The theorem is semantic and local to the arena's
self-readings.
\end{remark}

Metaphysically: an involutive arena that can name its own escapes cannot
close those names into a total self-surjection. Forced growth
(``emanation'' as naming the dodge) is the positive face of the same
fact. Concretely, one builds an $\omega$-ladder: level~$0$ is the
fundamental carrier; each successor adjoins a namer for the previous
level's dodge; history embeds injectively and conservatively; no level
exhausts its successor. Duration, in this sense, is the order-type of
self-escape. Direction along the ladder remains free
($\square(\mathsf{ascent})\land\neg\square(\mathsf{direction})$): ascent
is forced, which escape is named is not. The same pattern---forced
relation, free absolute---returns in Sections~\ref{sec:z2}
and~\ref{sec:transfer}.


\section{One underdetermination, three faces}
\label{sec:z2}

\begin{theorem}[One $\Ztwo$, three faces]
\label{thm:z2}
There is a single two-element underdetermination with three named
involutive projections:
\begin{center}
pole swap (reading) \quad$\cong$\quad
address $\{\mathsf{fwd},\mathsf{rev}\}$ \quad$\cong$\quad
channel $\{\varphi,\sigma\}$.
\end{center}
Each projection is an involution on a common $\Ztwo$ carrier.
\end{theorem}

The philosophical load is carried by \emph{stating the group once}.
Repeating $\Ztwo$ in three vocabularies without identifying them would
be concordance; identifying them without a theorem would be rhetoric.
Theorem~\ref{thm:z2} is the identification.

Section~\ref{sec:transfer} sharpens the channel face: absolute channel
position is unreal (canonicity fails), while channel \emph{exchange} is
real (swap covers reversal). That is the same relational pattern the
spine wears at $\neg\square(\mathsf{direction})$ and that cut-blind
modular dynamics wear when period is global: absolutes are refused;
relations survive.


\section{Transferring losslessness: two bounces}
\label{sec:transfer}

Spine acts are involutions. A ledger microstep is a forward map
$U:S\times E\to S\times E$. The interface demand (I-1) is that global
injectivity of $U$ be the import of Proposition~\ref{prop:no-erase} onto
that forward dynamics.

\begin{definition}[Two-bounce factorisation]
A \emph{two-bounce factorisation} of $U:\alpha\to\alpha$ is a pair of
involutions $(\phi,\sigma)$ with $U=\sigma\comp\phi$ pointwise.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Converse]
\label{thm:converse}
If $U$ admits a two-bounce factorisation, then $U$ is lossless
(injective). On a product carrier this is ledger injectivity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Existence on finite carriers]
\label{thm:exists}
Every injective $U:\mathrm{Fin}\,n\to\mathrm{Fin}\,n$ admits a
two-bounce factorisation. The construction is cycle-wise reflection at a
least-value basepoint, with $\sigma:=U\comp\phi$.
\end{theorem}

Existence plus converse discharges the interface shape: on finite
carriers, injectivity and two-bounce form coincide. Toys
(involutions as $U\comp\mathrm{id}$; Boolean injections; a swap step)
sit inside the same package.

\subsection{Canonicity fails}

\begin{theorem}[Canonicity fails]
\label{thm:canonfail}
There exist an injective $U:\Bool\to\Bool$ and two two-bounce
factorisations that disagree on $\phi$ (equivalently: $\neg$ factors as
both $\neg\comp\mathrm{id}$ and $\mathrm{id}\comp\neg$).
Moreover, on $\mathrm{Fin}\,3$ two reflection-axis factorisations of a
$3$-cycle disagree and are not related by bounce-swap
$(\phi,\sigma)\mapsto(\sigma,\phi)$.
\end{theorem}

In the dihedral picture, a rotation determines the angle between its two
mirrors, not their absolute position. Theorem~\ref{thm:canonfail} is
that fact discretely: the step fixes the relation between the bounces;
where the mirror pair sits is convention. Absolute channel position is
unreal; relative channel structure is real.

\subsection{Channel-swap is reversal}

Non-uniqueness does not block a proved channel column. Section~III never
needed canonical factors; it needed a canonical \emph{swap}.

\begin{theorem}[Channel-swap is reversal]
\label{thm:swap}
Let $U=\sigma\comp\phi$ with $\phi,\sigma$ involutions. Then
$\phi\comp\sigma$ is a two-sided inverse of $U$, and $(\sigma,\phi)$ is
a two-bounce presentation of that reverse step. The construction is
independent of which factorisation of $U$ was chosen.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
From $U=\sigma\comp\phi$ and the involution equations,
$\phi(\sigma(U(x)))=\phi(\sigma(\sigma(\phi(x))))=\phi(\phi(x))=x$ and
$U(\phi(\sigma(x)))=\sigma(\phi(\phi(\sigma(x))))=\sigma(\sigma(x))=x$.
The swapped pair $(\sigma,\phi)$ satisfies
$(\phi\comp\sigma)=\phi\comp\sigma$ definitionally as a two-bounce
presentation of the reverse step. No uniqueness of $(\phi,\sigma)$ was
used.
\end{proof}

On an involution, the reverse step equals $U$ itself, so factor-swap
re-presents the same step---the T-7-shaped reorder already visible in
$\neg\comp\mathrm{id}$ versus $\mathrm{id}\comp\neg$. In general,
channel-swap and address-swap (reading the step forward versus reverse)
project to the same inversion $U\mapsto U^{-1}$. The corpus cannot say
which mirror is which, and can prove that swapping them is running the
step backwards.


\section{Content gauges and packaging gauges}
\label{sec:gauge}

Gauge inflation is the natural hostile-reader objection once
factorisation non-uniqueness is on the table: has a second $\Ztwo$
appeared beside T-7? The answer is no, once a demarcation is stated.

\begin{definition}[Content vs packaging]
A gauge is \emph{content} when some exported statement mentions the
choice. A gauge is \emph{packaging} when the choice is quotiented before
anything downstream consumes it.
\end{definition}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Gauge & Locus & Class \\
\midrule
Label / pole swap & canon readings & content \\
Address fwd/rev & recovery / arrow line & content \\
Channel swap & Theorem~\ref{thm:swap} & content \\
Record gauge per tick & archive normal form & packaging \\
Factorisation gauge per step & Theorems~\ref{thm:exists}--\ref{thm:canonfail} & packaging \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The ledger consumes $U$, never the pair $(\phi,\sigma)$. Axis choice on
a $3$-cycle is spent entirely inside I-1 discharge. Record gauge appears
only inside ``up to record gauge'' clauses. By contrast, pole, address,
and channel swap survive into the dictionary line and the arrow
identification, so they are content---and they are the \emph{same}
content, stated once as Theorem~\ref{thm:z2}.

This demarcation has been used twice before being named (record gauge;
factorisation gauge). Naming it makes the one-$\Ztwo$ claim
hostile-reader-proof: the gauge grew, and it grew in a direction that
never touches the dictionary.


\section{Residue and refusals}
\label{sec:refuse}

Not everything nearby is a theorem, and the paper's credibility depends
on saying so.

\paragraph{Philosophical residue.}
The facticity of liveness---why there is a live act at all---is not
discharged. Ambient adequacy (whether pointed unoriented acts are the
right setting for fundamentality) remains contestable. These are
residue, not hidden axioms of the theorems above.

\paragraph{Refused.}
Premise-free global laws of time; continuum spacetime and continuum
Einstein equations as theorems of this corpus; host-logic incompleteness
and G\"odel arithmetisation of the formal text; experiential or qualia
claims. Dictionary instantiations (physical models admitted by
certificate) are existence proofs that the kernel symmetry has witnesses,
not a derivation of the world from $\Bool$.

\paragraph{Extension, not the thesis.}
A ledger layer (registration, capacity, discrete Einstein skeleton) and
dictionary certificates sit above the arena. They illustrate transfer and
constraint; they are not required for Theorems~\ref{thm:canon}--\ref{thm:swap}.
The present paper isolates the metaphysical core.


\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:concl}

An involutive arena yields a short list of hard theorems: a Boolean
canon; a Lawvere seal; one $\Ztwo$ with three faces; a discharged
losslessness transfer whose factorisation is non-canonical; and a proved
identification of channel-swap with step reversal. The accompanying
demarcation between content and packaging gauges prevents
non-uniqueness from counting as a second underdetermination.

Taken together, the strengths are not merely additive. Initiality gives
a unique fundamental \emph{object}; the seal gives a negative
\emph{epistemic} bound on self-reading; T-7 gives a unique
\emph{underdetermination}; two-bounce gives a discharged
\emph{interface}; canonicity failure plus swap-as-reversal give a
\emph{relational} reading of the channel face that matches the spine's
refusal of absolute direction. The gauge demarcation is what makes the
package stable under hostile reading.

The system keeps refusing absolutes at every level anyone proposes
one---direction of ascent, position of mirrors, labels of channels---and
the refusals were found by attempting the opposite. That is the strongest
kind of negative result available to formal metaphysics: not a slogan, but
a failed canonicity proof, machine-checked, that promotes the exchange it
could not eliminate.
